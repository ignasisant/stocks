The Portfolio page measures performance in two different ways and risk in a separate way. Which number answers which question is the main thing to get right.

## Time-weighted return (TWR): "Return on your picks"
The time-weighted return (TWR) on the Portfolio page measures how your investments performed while ignoring when you put money in or took it out. As a general definition, TWR chains the daily returns of the book, so a deposit or withdrawal is not counted as a gain or a loss. The app builds it from the ledger's daily value in your reporting currency: each day's return is the day's value change after removing that day's buys and sells, divided by the previous day's value. Sold positions, dividends and currency moves are included. Because timing is left out, it is the figure to compare with an index. It is shown as "Return on your picks" on the Overview tab and as "Yearly return" in the Real performance card on the Allocation & risk tab, where the IRR sits beside it as a pill ("Your timing"). The annualised TWR is the compound daily growth scaled to a year.

## Money-weighted return (IRR): "Return on your money"
The money-weighted return (IRR, internal rate of return) on the Portfolio page measures what your own money earned, so the timing of your deposits and withdrawals does matter. As a general definition, IRR is the single yearly rate that makes the money you put in, each amount on its exact date, grow into today's value. The app treats every buy and sale on its exact trade date as a cash flow, the book's value at the start of the window as the buy-in and today's value as the final payoff. The headline "Return on your money" tile is expressed as a yearly rate. In the month-by-month chart on the Overview tab, the lines are cumulative (not annualised) during the first year of the book, because a few months stretched to a year exaggerate any result; from the book's first birthday they become annual rates, with no jump. TWR and IRR often differ: that gap shows the effect of your timing.

## Volatility and maximum drawdown of your actual returns
On the Allocation & risk tab, the Real performance card shows the annualised volatility and the maximum drawdown of your actual book. Volatility is the standard deviation of the daily TWR returns, scaled to a year: how bumpy the ride was. The maximum drawdown is the worst fall from a peak to the lowest point after it, measured on the cumulative TWR path, so deposits and withdrawals do not distort it. Every day weighs the same whatever the book was worth, so a fall when the book was small counts as much as one on today's book; the app shows the peak and trough dates of that fall when there is one. It is not money lost on what you hold today. Its tiles are called "Real swings" and "Real max drawdown". The Period selector (Since start, 6M, 1Y, 2Y, 5Y) drives both cards on the tab and shows the dates it spans; the allocation donuts are always today's.

## Real performance vs Risk of today's holdings (simulated)
The Allocation & risk tab splits into two cards that answer different questions. "Real performance" describes what your account actually did: its figures come from the ledger's daily value path, with your real trades, sold positions, dividends and currency moves. "Risk of today's holdings (simulated)" describes what you hold today: the app takes today's holdings at today's weights, assumes they were held unchanged over the window, and measures simulated swings (volatility), simulated max drawdown, effective diversification, the weight of your 5 largest positions and beta. It is a historical simulation of the current composition, not a forecast and not a loss you actually took. The two disagree by as much as your book has changed shape over time. The window starts at your first trade by default, so it never scores years in which you held nothing.

## Benchmarks and beta
The Portfolio page compares your book with three reference indices, shown through the ETFs SPY (US large caps), QQQ (US tech) and EEM (emerging markets), converted to your reporting currency. In the chart "Your gain vs the indexes" (labelled S&P 500, Nasdaq 100 and Emerging markets), each benchmark is a shadow account: it receives your own cash flows on the same dates (a buy puts the same money into the index, a sale takes the same money out), and every line is drawn as the money it has gained or lost, in your currency, over the net money put in. This avoids crediting an index with all your money from day one, and keeps a big percentage swing on the first few hundred euros from dwarfing later years. The sentence above the chart says your gain and how far it is from the best index. The "If you'd always held today's mix" line, off until you press "Compare with today's holdings", receives the same flows into today's holdings at today's weights. Beta vs a benchmark says how much your basket moves when the index moves: 1.2 means a 1% index move tends to be 1.2% in the basket. Beta is measured on the current basket.

## Five-year projection: how the scenarios work
The Projection tab on the Portfolio page shows a range for what your book could be worth in 1, 3, 5 (default) or 10 years. It is not a prediction: the app runs 4,000 simulated scenarios from today's value, month by month, and shows the central scenario (p50), the pessimistic and optimistic edges (p10 and p90, so 8 in 10 scenarios end inside the range) and the middle half (p25 to p75). The book is simulated as two separate blocks, stocks and crypto, each with its own growth rate and its own volatility measured from your current holdings over the last two years (if that cannot be measured, the app falls back to 20% a year for stocks and 60% for crypto). The two blocks are correlated as they were over the same period. The same inputs always give the same fan. Taxes and fees are not included.

## Projection: growth rates, index references and your own rate
In the Projection tab, the growth you choose is a median compound annual return, the meaning of "8% a year". For stocks the presets are 4%, 6%, MSCI World ~8% (the default), S&P 500 ~10% and Nasdaq-100 ~13%; the index ones approximate long-run nominal records with dividends and are a reference, not a promise. "Your stocks" and "Your crypto" instead repeat the compound growth of your current holdings over the same window (up to 2 years); they are only offered when at least a year of history exists, and a good stretch projected years ahead is a hypothesis, not a plan. For crypto the options are -20%, 0% (the default), 10% and 25% a year, because there is no long run to read a rate from.

## Projection: contributions, target and nominal vs real
In the Projection tab you can add a monthly contribution, which is added at the end of each month and split between stocks and crypto. The box starts at zero, and next to it the app shows your last-12-months average of net money put in (buys less sales) as a hint. The share of contributions going to crypto defaults to crypto's weight in your book today and can be set to 0%, 10% or 25%. If you type a target amount, the app shows the chance of reaching it: the share of simulated scenarios that end the horizon at or above it. The "Amounts" switch chooses Nominal figures or "In today's money", which deflates the future at 2% a year inflation (the ECB's target, not a forecast); the past part of the chart stays in the money of its day.
