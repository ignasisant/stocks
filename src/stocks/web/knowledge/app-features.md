The full description of every page and feature, one section per stop of the guided tour. The tour itself shows a two-line summary; this is the detail behind it.

## Welcome to TopStocks
TopStocks turns a broker statement into your real numbers: FIFO lots, P/L in your own currency at the ECB rate of each trade's own date, and what your tax office will want. This tour walks through each feature, says whether your account has it switched on, and takes you to it. Two minutes, and you can leave and come back at any point.

## Import your transactions
Drop a statement: every row is parsed and previewed before anything is written to your ledger. Revolut (CSV and PDF), Revolut crypto, Trading 212, DEGIRO, Interactive Brokers, ClickTrade / Saxo and a generic CSV are recognised. **Any crypto exchange's export** works too — Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, Bitpanda, Crypto.com, Koinly or whichever wrote it: just drop the file, the assistant works out its columns, and coin-to-coin swaps, staking rewards and deposits are valued at that day's price. Re-importing the same statement never duplicates a row. If your browser or work device blocks the file picker, **paste the statement text** instead — same parse, same preview, same commit. No statement to hand yet? **Load an example statement** puts a real one through the same parse, preview and commit, and **Clear last import** takes it back out again. Not ready to hand over a real statement? **Use demo transactions** on the Portfolio page fills the ledger with a made-up book of seven well-known stocks so every tab has something in it; it is labelled as demo wherever it shows, and your first real import deletes it. Broker not listed? **Type your positions instead** (on Import and on the demo banner) opens the assistant with the start of a sentence in the box, like "I hold: 10 NVDA at 120, bought 2024-03-01"; it books what you type once you confirm, and that first trade removes the demo book like an import would. Shares moved between brokers are recognised even when the two statements name the company differently (an ISIN at one, the symbol at the other) or the arrival reads as a plain buy, and booking them as a move can be undone. Everything else in the app — positions, P/L, allocation, tax, dividends, fees — derives from this one ledger.

## Positions and custody
Your open lots with live prices, market value and weight in your reporting currency, plus which broker each holding actually sits in — a ticker split across two custodians shows as a split. Every ticker cell links through to its full page.

## Real return and risk
Two different questions, two sections. **Real return** is what your book actually did: time-weighted return (flow-adjusted, so a deposit never reads as a gain), money-weighted return (IRR), volatility and worst drawdown. **Risk of the current basket** is about what you hold today — allocation by sector, geography, currency and broker.

## Five-year projection
A range, not a prediction: 4,000 simulated scenarios from today's value, with **stocks and crypto moved separately** — each at its own real volatility and the correlation the two have shown. Stock growth is picked by **index reference** (MSCI World, S&P 500, Nasdaq-100) or, if you pick it, **your own holdings' rate** over the last two years — never by default; crypto starts at zero. Add a **monthly contribution** or a **target** and read the central case, the range and the chance of getting there, nominal or in today's money.

## Realized gains and tax
Sales matched against your lots — FIFO, LIFO, average cost or UK S104, whichever your country uses — realized gains per year net of commissions, and your jurisdiction's own rules on top. Twelve are implemented (ES, US, UK, DE, FR, IT, IE, PT, CA, AU, AE, CH), each with its own brackets, allowances, reporting flags and reporting currency. Set your tax residence in Profile: it decides which rules run and which currency the ledger is replayed in.

## Dividends and broker costs
Dividends per year and per position, with withholding and the credit you can claim — and, beside them, what your shares were **entitled** to: every company's dividend history priced by the shares your ledger held the day before each ex-date, so a payment your statement never carried still shows up, and next year's income is estimated on today's holdings. On Home, those payments no statement carried join **Recent transactions** too, summed into one row marked as an estimate that opens onto each payment. The **Fees** tab then separates what the broker charged from what the spread cost you: each execution is measured against the midpoint of its own session, so the markup that never appears on a statement becomes a number.

## Sell or add
Every holding scored on **business quality** and on **price**, then weighed against the rest of the book: what to sell, what to trim because it has grown too big or too risky, and what to add to. Each move comes sized in money, with the **tax** a sale adds this year and the date a buy stops undoing a loss sold inside the repurchase window. Below, your watchlist's names and any ticker you type are judged **buy, watch or pass** on the same scale. Both tables show each name's **return** over one month, six months, a year or two years (pick the span beside the search; the link keeps it), in your own currency with dividends, and how many points it ran ahead of or behind the S&P 500 and its sector's ETF over the same days. A screen over public fundamentals, not advice. It needs a book of your own: until your first import (the example portfolio does not count) the page shows a blurred example of itself with an **Import portfolio** button.

## The daily briefing
One AI card on Home, generated once a day (it turns over at 09:00 in your own timezone). Home shows its headline and first few lines, fired alerts first, and **Read Today** opens the whole card on the Today page: a headline naming the day's few things, then three parts by default. **Portfolio** sets your book's move today, this week and this month beside the S&P 500's, with the month drawn. **Today's alerts** are your own alerts the price crossed in the latest session — one that fired days ago is not on the card. **Worth a look** has a line for each thing that came up: a print on a name you hold or watch in the next two weeks and how the latest ones did against the estimate, a **Fed or ECB** decision around the corner, the **tax calendar** (a filing deadline, the year closing with gains or losses still open, the next savings bracket, the day a sold loss can be bought back), positions at a 52-week edge and **the market your book sits in**. The **pencil** in the card's corner changes that: write what it should tell you each day, a line per thing, and each line becomes a section of its own, in your order, under your fired alerts — your biggest moves, the **events of the next seven days** (results, ex-dividend dates, the Fed and the ECB, tax deadlines), **insider trades** at your largest holdings (SEC Form 4, BaFin for German issuers), the last days' **headlines** naming your companies, the **8-Ks** they filed this week, which **big investors** added to or cut them last quarter (13F, dated), a review of your **exit criteria** against your alerts and notes, the main indices, rates or crypto, a long-term proposal marked as one. Start from a template or your own words; it reads back what it will fetch before the first card, and a line it has no data for says so rather than call the day quiet. The chat keeps the same list when you ask it for something every day. It **remembers what it told you**: a trigger it already showed stays off until its figure moves or its date gets closer. Each line opens its own **analysis**: the comparison it invites, made — the company against its peers, its sector and the S&P 500, its business figures and the analysts' consensus, what a sale saves in tax, which positions explain a month behind the index — as a verdict, a few points and the tables behind them, and every figure in the prose is checked against what was fetched. If no model answers it falls back to the same facts and tables without the prose. Home's cards are yours to order: **Customise** moves, hides and adds them, and the order follows your account to every device.

## Today
The day's page, written each morning for your book (Today in the menu, and in the mobile bar). Two layers. **Your part** is written when you open it, with the same daily card as Home and nothing more spent: your fired alerts, your book against the S&P 500, your routines, then three computed sections. **News** has the last days' Yahoo headlines naming your largest companies, their SEC 8-K filings this week and their latest results, each linked to its source; a headline is a quote, never advice. **Insiders** has open-market buys and sells by directors at your largest holdings over 30 days (SEC Form 4, BaFin for German issuers). **Comparisons** sets each position's last month, six months and year against its sector ETF and the S&P 500, in percentage points, with the Sell or add verdict (pick the span above the table); a fund or a coin, which has no sector, against the S&P 500 only. The AI adds at most one note per section, and only on a notable gap. **The market part** is the same for every account and written once a weekday morning before the open: the US 10-year yield, the 2-10 curve and the high-yield spread with their change over a month, the Fed and the ECB with their rate and the days to their next decision, US and euro-area inflation, the risk-appetite regime, how many sector ETFs hold their 100-session average, which sectors lead and trail the S&P 500 over the month, and crypto fear & greed with bitcoin dominance. Tabs filter the page (Alerts, Your book, News, Insiders, Comparisons, Macro, Trends, Routines), and the link keeps the tab. A source that did not answer is named under its section rather than the section being left out. Things already said stay off until they change: a headline is told once, an insider buy again only if the amount moves or a cluster appears. Signed out, the page shows the market part only.

## Your watchlist
The list every other page reads: Overview, Screener, Earnings and the ticker picker all run on it. Add a name by **searching** it — your own list, coins, funds, the SEC company map and every venue Yahoo quotes, so a symbol arrives with its real name instead of being spelled from memory. **Groups** are the app's grouping primitive: a ticker can sit in several, the list can be grouped by them, and Overview renders a section per group. Everything you edit here writes immediately.

## Market pulse
Where the market is heading, read against your own book — ranked by how much of it you actually need. The top card answers it in five seconds: **risk appetite** as a 0-100 score on its own meter, with a ghost pin where the score stood a month ago, and beside it the same regime restated as what it does to your basket. **Why** opens the number up: all eight inputs — trend, breadth, volatility, term structure, credit, stocks-versus-bonds, junk demand and emerging markets — each with its own bar, a tick at where it stood a month ago and its raw level, so the score is auditable rather than a black box; an input that could not be built is named and greyed rather than quietly dropped. Alongside it, four readings that only exist as trends: how many indices and sectors are above their own trend, the stock/bond correlation (positive means both legs fall together), and which of the four rates regimes the last quarter describes. **Your book against this regime** measures your basket's sensitivity to equities, rates, credit and the dollar — each pill is the three-month drift, which is the regime moving under you without your having traded. **The detail** keeps the six long tables — indices, gauges, rates, inflation, rotation and cross-asset — behind one tab strip, every row the same shape: the level, the change over 1w / 1m / 3m / 12m, a sparkline, and whether the series is in an uptrend or turning. Every row there carries an **i** beside its name that says what the series is and how to read it, because “MOVE” and “2s10s” are not names that explain themselves.

## Any ticker, with its sources
Price and chart, from one day to its whole listed history, with a zoom range that reports the move over exactly the window you selected and a measure between any two dates (two fingers on a phone, a right-click on a computer), plus financials, estimates, what **analysts** say (their rating, price targets and which way they are revising earnings), insider activity and news. Under the price, **Compare** opens with the listing over the S&P 500, its sector's ETF and a few related companies; chips add more related names and the search anything else (the Nasdaq 100 or emerging markets included), up to six lines, each as its total return in your own currency since the first day of the window (one month to two years), so which did better is simply which line ends higher; a listing with no price history or no exchange rate into your currency is named under the chart rather than drawn in the wrong money. The related names offered first are the same industry's from the sector screen's nightly cohort, then Yahoo's "people also follow". **Comparables** lays the KPIs of the names you pick side by side, with medals for the best composite rank, a **peer median** column (the rivals' median and their middle half, P25 – P75, the company itself left out) and a • on any figure more than 3σ from the others' median. The **Fundamentals** grid reads each company on its own lens: a bank on P/TBV, ROTCE, dividend yield and payout instead of EV/EBITDA and ROIC; a REIT or a utility on yield, payout and EV/EBITDA, with net debt judged against its industry's line and a landlord's P/E and new shares shown without a verdict. Under each tile, **Peers** gives the rivals' median (the same industry from the nightly scan, else the sector), and when margins sit at a peak the P/E also shows what it would be at a normal margin. A multiple at zero or below gets no verdict and a negative P/E reads **Loss**. Under the grid, **Watch** lists the review engine's cautions for the name (peak margin, heavy dilution, earnings not backed by cash…). The header names what the symbol is — stock, equity fund, bond fund, money market, closed-end fund, crypto or index — and the page shows the figures that kind is read by: a money-market fund shows its yield against the central bank's rate, not an RSI. Every KPI carries where it came from: **Fact** is verifiable in a filing, **Consensus** is analyst estimates, **Derived** is computed here. The search box at the top of the page works from anywhere in the app and labels each result with its kind. **Analyze with AI** in the header hands the whole company to the assistant — one click asks it about the business, the fundamentals, the valuation against its own history and how the position fits your book (funds and coins get their own question; an index, which nobody holds directly, gets none). A coin's page is read as a coin: how far under its **all-time high** it trades, where it sits in its **cycle** (Fear & Greed, the Mayer multiple, the 200-week average, the halving clock), what perpetual-swap traders pay to hold it (**funding** and **open interest**) and, when you hold it, its share of your crypto and what selling it at a loss would save in tax.

- **Reconstructed P/E**: split-adjusted close over trailing-12-month diluted GAAP EPS rebuilt from quarterly filings, stepped forward on each filing date (each day uses only figures already public); loss-making stretches are omitted. It can differ from the P/E (TTM) in Fundamentals when the latest quarter is in Yahoo's trailing EPS but its 10-Q/10-K is not on SEC EDGAR yet, or because of adjusted vs GAAP EPS.
- **Annual chart**: hatched bars are consensus revenue, the dashed line consensus EPS with the low-high analyst range; years beyond published estimates are extrapolated (revenue at next-FY consensus growth, EPS at long-term growth).
- **German insiders (BaFin, MAR art. 19)**: officers, directors and close associates trading their own company's stock, notified within 3 business days, last 12 months. BaFin gives an average price and an aggregate euro volume, so the share count is derived (volume / price).
- **Closed-end funds**: a fixed number of shares trading above or below NAV. Figures come from the fund's SEC filings and Yahoo's NAV line; the expense ratio includes interest on leverage, and part of the distribution can be return of capital (Section 19 notices). No statements, insiders, earnings dates or comparables.

## Sector screen and earnings calendar
Pick a sector and see a comparable cohort of companies — the sector ETF's basket, widened with listed names outside the US — ranked on valuation and quality, with the best three called out and read for you. A second **best three** ranks the same cohort on technical momentum instead — trend, RSI, MACD, distance from its 52-week high, 3-month return and volume. Then see which of your holdings report next, beside the **tax deadlines** of your tax residence — flagged 30 days ahead — and the **ex-dividend dates** of what you hold, each with what it pays on your shares; last year's schedule fills the months nobody has declared yet. The same calendar marks the day a position sold at a loss can be **bought back** without deferring the loss, and the **Fed and ECB** rate decisions. Click any date and it says what it means for you — on a dividend, the cash that lands, net of the withholding your own statements show.

## The assistant
The chat panel on the right, reachable from every page, with your real book in hand: it reads your positions, live quotes, filings and the web, and picks the analysis skill that fits the question. A tax question gets the **Tax engine & lot methods** skill: it prices a sale or a loss harvest through the same engine as the Realized & tax tab, explains the lot method and repurchase rule of your tax residence, and always says it is an estimate, not tax advice. While it researches, each lookup is listed with what it is for; a longer research also shows its **plan**, the steps it means to take ticked off as it goes, and says what it could not find rather than filling the gap from memory. A question that needs more — or the **In depth** switch in the lens menu, for the next question only — gets a longer research that reads more sources and can take a couple of minutes; on the shared chain it counts as two messages. An empty thread opens with a few suggestions built from your own watchlist, so it has something useful to say before you have imported anything — and, until you have, a first row that takes you to **Import**. Without signing in you see the assistant but cannot use it: it opens on a sample answer about the example portfolio and a sign-in button, and a question you asked from a page is waiting in the box once you are signed in. It is there for investing, markets, your portfolio and the app: on the shared chain a question about anything else may get one line saying so instead of an answer. It runs keyless on the shared TopStocks chain within a daily cap; connect your own provider key for no cap and any model that provider offers, or **sign in with OpenRouter** and have one minted on your account without copying anything. You can ask out loud as well: press the microphone on the composer and what you say is written into the field as you say it, then checked against the recording when you stop, so you read and fix it before it goes — the audio itself is never stored. While an answer is being written the send button becomes a stop button: press it and the answer stops where it is, keeping whatever it had already written, and any lookup still running stops with it. A connection that drops mid-answer is not read as a stop: the question is answered again the next time you open the app. If the app itself restarts mid-answer, reopening the conversation picks the answer back up from where its research had got to, without looking anything up twice or counting the question again. Asked to change something — a favourite, an alert, a position — it shows what it understood and waits for **Confirm**, with **Edit** to fix a misheard price or symbol first. It may also offer one change of its own — an alert at a price it just found, a ticker for your watchlist — as a card under its answer that does nothing until you approve it. It can **fix your transactions** too — list, edit, delete or add rows, book a company two brokers spelled differently under one ticker, turn a sale and a buy into the **transfer** they were, or check the whole book for mistakes like these: each change is shown row by row with what it does to your holdings and realized gains before you confirm, and has an **Undo** after. On Telegram it waits for you to write "yes". A question that asks for a decision ("should I buy…?") is argued first by a **bull and a bear analyst**, and the answer weighs the two instead of taking the side the question leaned to. Ask for a chart — a share, a currency pair, an index, gold — and the app draws a **price chart** from real closes under the answer, with chips to switch the window, rather than the model sketching one from memory. Ask how you are doing against the S&P and **your own portfolio** is one of its lines, as its time-weighted return; ask how your book is split and it draws a **donut** by position, sector, country or currency, funds looked through. Asked which losses would offset this year's gains, it replays each through your tax rules and lists the **harvest candidates** with what each saves and the day a repurchase is clear; asked to take a position to a weight, it sizes the trade on a **target-weight slider** and prices the tax on the sale. It **remembers what you tell it about yourself** — say "remember that…", or mention a goal or a preference in passing — and each memory it saves, corrects or drops is shown under the answer with **Undo**; name something you discussed in an earlier conversation and that conversation is brought into the answer, with a link back to it. The list lives in **Settings → Memory** (and on your Profile), where you can edit it or switch it off.

## Decision journal
A record of what the app proposed, written by the app and never by you: nothing in it asks why you bought anything. Every move it actually **showed** you lands here — a sale, trim or harvest on the daily card, a verdict on **Sell or add**, an alert or a watchlist change offered in the chat — with the reasons given, the figures it was made on (quality and price scores, weight, P&L, the loss to harvest, the alert level) and what came of it: **open** while it is still being proposed, **followed** or **declined** when you answered it in the chat — or **followed** on its own when a sale or buy of that name turns up in your book within 30 days of it being shown, nothing asked — **lapsed** when the page stopped proposing it or 30 days went by without it being shown again. A proposal shown again is the same entry, counted once a day; a different move on the same thing (a trim that became a sell) replaces it and the two are linked. Read it whole in **Profile → Journal**, filtered by state, by where it was proposed and by ticker; on a ticker's page a **Journal** chip in the header opens that name's entries in a side panel. A month and again a quarter after a sale, trim, add or buy was first shown, the app reads the price back and keeps the move since, whatever you did with it, so you can see whether the name went the way the move bet on. The tab opens with the track record (how many moves were proposed, taken, turned down, lapsed, and how many of those priced went their way) and a **Download Markdown** link: the whole journal as one file a notes app such as Obsidian reads, with tags and links between entries. The daily card, the assistant and the Claude connector read it too, so a move you declined is not pressed again in the same words.

## Telegram notifications
A portfolio digest in the evening and price alerts during market hours, on Telegram. The digest ranks the day by what each holding did to your book in your reporting currency rather than by the loudest percentage, sets it against the S&P 500, and lists the week's ex-dividend dates and the payments already received — and, once, any tax deadline coming up in the next 30 days. Link your account once from the Notifications tab and the toggles start working; the jobs run server-side, so nothing depends on you having the app open.

## Your investor profile
Risk tolerance, horizon, focus and constraints. The assistant reads it before answering, so the analysis is framed for your mandate instead of a generic one. The **focus** areas do one more thing: the watchlist tab then offers example tickers from them that you do not already follow — examples to look at, never recommendations, and adding them removes nothing you have.

## Preferences
Reporting currency, language, tax residence and the bracket inputs your jurisdiction reads, grouped into three cards; the tax card states what the choice decides (cost currency, share matching, tax year) instead of describing it. Nothing here has a Save button — every change is written as you make it. It is also where the ledger exports to CSV, where an account deletes itself, and where this tour lives whenever you want it again. The **Claude** card holds the connector address that lets Claude read your book, and lists every app connected to it with a **Revoke** beside each.
