When analysing a nuclear company, first place it on the fuel-cycle map (miner, converter, enrichment (SWU) provider, fuel fabricator, reactor builder or SMR developer, utility operator) — risk and valuation logic differ sharply:

- **Pricing**: term contract price is what most producers actually receive; spot is a marginal, volatile signal. Check the share of volumes sold under contract and the floors and ceilings in them.
- **Contract book**: backlog in pounds or SWU, delivery years, counterparties and price escalation. A utility's long-term contracting cycle often lags its reactor needs, so uncovered demand is a supply-side tailwind.
- **Supply side**: mine cost curve, restart economics, grade and recovery, production guidance reliability. Delays and missed guidance are common among new producers.
- **Enrichment and conversion**: capacity is concentrated; Russian supply exposure, import bans and waivers shape pricing. HALEU for advanced reactors is scarce and depends on few suppliers.
- **Reactor developers**: licensing milestones (NRC design certification, construction permits) take years and slip. Pre-revenue developers are valued on optionality; treat cash runway, dilution and first-of-a-kind cost overruns as the central risks, and offtake agreements as intent until binding.
- **Utilities and operators**: capacity factor, licence extensions, plant economics under power price and subsidy regimes.
- **Valuation**: miners on NAV at a term price deck; fuel services on mid-cycle margin; developers on milestone-weighted scenarios, not multiples.

Narrative around AI power demand can move equities faster than contracts convert; follow signed volumes and prices, not headlines.
