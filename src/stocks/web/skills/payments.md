When analysing a payments or credit-services company, first place it on the map (card network, acquirer or processor, issuer and lender, buy-now-pay-later, wallet or fintech) — risk and margin structure differ greatly:

- **Networks**: total payment volume growth, cross-border volume (higher yield), transaction count, and net revenue yield; capital-light and high margin, with no credit risk.
- **Acquirers and processors**: gross payment volume, take rate and its trend, merchant mix, and net revenue after interchange. Take-rate compression is the main threat from competition and large merchants.
- **Lenders and issuers**: loan growth, net interest margin, net charge-off rate and delinquencies, reserve build against the loan book. Benign credit flatters results; check late-cycle consumer stress.
- **BNPL**: loss rates by cohort, funding cost, merchant fees against credit loss, and regulation of short-term credit.
- **Regulation**: interchange caps, late-fee rules, and scrutiny of network fees; antitrust actions in the US and EU.
- **Competition**: account-to-account and real-time payments (Pix, UPI, instant transfers), wallets and big-tech players that bypass cards.
- **Funding and capital**: deposits vs securitisation, capital ratios for bank-like issuers.
- **Valuation**: P/E and FCF yield for networks; P/TBV against return on equity for lenders; EV/gross profit for processors. A fintech with credit risk on its own balance sheet is judged as a lender.

Volume growth is not the same as profit: ask what share of each additional dollar stays after rewards, losses and funding.
