When analysing a biotech, first place it by stage (preclinical, clinical-stage, newly launched, profitable commercial) and by concentration (single asset vs platform with several programmes) — the framework differs:

- **Cash runway**: cash and equivalents divided by quarterly burn, in quarters. Under roughly four quarters means a raise is near; runways reaching beyond key readouts are valuable.
- **Pipeline**: lead assets by phase, indication, modality and addressable market. As a rough guide, approval odds rise from around one in ten at Phase 1 to roughly half or more at Phase 3, varying by area.
- **Valuation**: risk-adjusted NPV (rNPV) per asset: peak sales, probability of success, launch year, discounting. Early assets deserve small weights; market cap well below cash is a signal of distrust, not free money.
- **Catalysts**: data readouts, regulatory filing and decision dates. Binary events can move a small cap by half; position for the downside.
- **Dilution**: at-the-market (ATM) programmes, follow-ons and warrants; check share count growth and who funds rounds.
- **Partnerships**: upfronts, milestones and royalties validate science and fund development, but milestones are conditional.
- **Single-asset risk**: one failure can remove most of the value; platform names spread it but burn more.
- **Commercial stage**: launch curve vs consensus, patient starts, payer coverage and pricing, gross-to-net, and path to profitability.

Positive early data often triggers an equity raise; judge a readout by what it does to the cost of capital as well as to the science.
