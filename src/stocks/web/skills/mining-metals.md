When analysing a mining or metals company, first place it on the map (precious metals miner, royalty/streaming, base-metals miner, steel and aluminium processor, coal) — earnings leverage and risk differ:

- **Cost position**: all-in sustaining cost (AISC) or cash cost against the spot price; margin per ounce or tonne drives free cash flow. Position on the industry cost curve decides survival in downturns.
- **Reserves and grade**: reserve life, grade trend, strip ratio and recovery; falling grades raise unit costs. Resources are not reserves.
- **Valuation**: price to net asset value (P/NAV) at a stated metal price deck; EV/EBITDA on mid-cycle prices. P/E at peak prices overstates value.
- **Jurisdiction risk**: royalty hikes, export bans, nationalisation and permitting in some countries; weigh asset mix by location.
- **Capex and projects**: overruns and delays are common in new mines; check development stage, funding and inflation in labour and energy.
- **By-product credits**: copper-gold or silver-zinc mines report lower costs through credits, which can distort headline costs; compare with and without.
- **Royalties and streams**: low-cost exposure to metal price and exploration upside, with no operating cost; judge on asset quality and counterparty.
- **Steel, aluminium, coal**: spreads over raw materials, energy costs, trade protection and demand from construction and autos.

Miners earn the most when prices are high and capital discipline is weakest; watch for a spending spree with the price near its peak.
