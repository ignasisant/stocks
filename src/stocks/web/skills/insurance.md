When analysing an insurer, first place it on the map (property and casualty, reinsurer, life and annuity, health, broker) — the metrics and valuation differ and standard P/E can mislead:

- **P&C underwriting**: combined ratio (loss plus expense), roughly below 100% means an underwriting profit. Check prior-year reserve development: repeated releases flatter results, adverse development warns of under-reserving.
- **Catastrophe exposure**: share of premium in cat-prone lines, reinsurance protection, and the pricing cycle (hard markets raise rates, soft markets erode them). A quiet cat year is not skill.
- **Life**: embedded value or, under IFRS 17, contractual service margin (CSM) and its new business contribution; persistency, guarantees and interest-rate sensitivity of long-dated liabilities.
- **Solvency and capital**: Solvency II ratio for European names (and management target and sensitivities to rates and spreads), RBC for US; excess capital funds dividends and buybacks.
- **Float and investments**: premiums held until claims are paid earn investment yield; check portfolio duration, credit quality and unrealised losses.
- **Brokers**: fee businesses with little underwriting risk; judge by organic growth, margin and acquisition pace, not by combined ratio.
- **Valuation**: price/book or price/tangible book against return on equity for P&C; price/embedded value for life; EV/EBITDA for brokers.

An insurer can report good earnings while its reserves quietly weaken; trace reserve changes before trusting a clean combined ratio.
