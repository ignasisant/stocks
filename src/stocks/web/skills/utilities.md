When analysing a utility, first place it on the map (regulated networks and vertically integrated utilities, renewables developer, independent power producer or merchant generator, water, gas distribution) — earnings drivers differ:

- **Rate base and growth**: EPS growth tracks rate base growth when regulators allow returns. Check the capex plan size, its visibility and where the growth is (grid, renewables, nuclear, data-center connections).
- **Allowed vs earned return**: allowed ROE set by the regulator against what is actually earned; regulatory lag (time between spending and recovery) erodes the gap. Constructive jurisdictions earn a higher multiple than adversarial ones.
- **Funding the plan**: capex above operating cash flow needs debt and equity issuance. Equity issuance dilutes; look at the FFO/debt ratio against rating thresholds.
- **Payout and yield**: payout ratio typically in a stated range; a high yield funded by new debt is weak. Utilities trade partly as bond proxies, so valuation moves inversely with long-term yields.
- **Merchant exposure**: IPPs and generators earn on power prices and capacity markets; contracted data-center or industrial supply agreements reduce volatility, but check tenor, pricing and counterparty.
- **Weather and rates**: demand sensitivity to weather, and refinancing cost on large debt stacks.
- **Risks**: wildfire and storm liability, political pressure on bills, reserve margin and permitting delays.
- **Valuation**: P/E relative to regulated peers, dividend yield spread over the bond yield, and sum-of-parts when regulated and merchant units are mixed.

Rising rates hurt twice: they raise the cost of funding the plan and lower the multiple investors pay for the yield.
