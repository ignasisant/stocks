When analysing a semiconductor company, first place it on the map (fabless designer, IDM, foundry, equipment/materials supplier, analog/power vs digital/AI accelerators) — cycle behaviour and moats differ by segment:

- **Cycle position**: book-to-bill, lead times and pricing in the segment. Memory and commodity analog swing hardest; leading-edge logic and equipment swing with customer capex. Say whether the cycle is early, mid or late.
- **Inventory**: days of inventory at the company and at its customers. Rising channel inventory with flat sell-through precedes revenue cuts; double-ordering inflates peak numbers.
- **Equipment demand**: wafer-fab equipment (WFE) follows foundry and memory capex, with a lag; service revenue from the installed base smooths it. Check China share of sales.
- **Competitive position**: design wins and their ramp timing, process node and packaging leadership, software or ecosystem lock-in. Fabless names depend on foundry capacity; IDMs and foundries carry heavy fixed costs and utilisation risk.
- **Customer concentration**: a few hyperscalers or one device maker can be most of revenue; in-house chips by those customers are the standing threat. Order digestion after a buildout is common.
- **Policy**: export controls, licences and tariffs can remove a market overnight; separate the revenue at risk from the headline.
- **Valuation**: P/E on peak margins misleads. Use mid-cycle earnings or EV/sales against through-cycle margin, and FCF after heavy capex for fabs.

Semiconductors often look cheapest on trailing earnings just before the downturn and most expensive just after the trough.
