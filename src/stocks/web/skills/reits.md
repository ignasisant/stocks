When analysing a REIT or real estate company, first place it by property type and model (offices, logistics, retail, residential, data centers, towers, healthcare, developer vs landlord) — fundamentals and rate sensitivity differ:

- **Earnings measure**: use FFO and AFFO (after recurring capex), not EPS, which is distorted by depreciation and revaluations. Payout against AFFO shows whether the dividend is covered.
- **Operating quality**: occupancy, weighted average lease expiry (WALE), tenant mix and concentration, rent reversion on renewal, and same-store NOI growth. Indexation clauses protect income in inflation.
- **Valuation**: discount or premium to net asset value (NAV) and the implied cap rate against recent transactions and the bond yield; AFFO yield against the cost of debt.
- **Balance sheet**: loan-to-value, interest cover, share of fixed-rate debt, and the maturity ladder; refinancing at higher rates lowers earnings with a lag, while appraisals fall with cap-rate expansion.
- **Sub-type specifics**: data centers hinge on power availability and hyperscaler leases; towers on tenant escalators and churn; logistics on rent growth and supply; offices on hybrid-work vacancy and capex to re-let.
- **Spanish SOCIMI**: tax-regime payout rules and the minimum holding period shape distributions; liquidity is often thin.
- **Developers**: land bank, pre-sales and leverage matter more than FFO.

Rates drive the whole group; a high dividend yield with a falling NAV and rising LTV is a value trap more often than a bargain.
