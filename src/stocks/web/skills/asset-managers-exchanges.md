When analysing an asset manager, broker, exchange or financial data provider, first place it on the map (traditional active manager, passive/ETF provider, alternatives manager, wealth platform, investment bank or broker, exchange, data and ratings provider) — earnings drivers differ:

- **Asset managers**: assets under management (AUM), organic net flows (separate from market moves), and fee rate; active managers face outflows and fee compression to passive products, while alternatives managers earn on fee-paying AUM and lock-ups.
- **Performance fees**: lumpy and market-dependent; discount them, and value base management fees at a higher multiple.
- **Market beta**: earnings correlate with equity markets and activity; a rally lifts AUM and trading, a decline reverses it. Operating leverage cuts both ways.
- **Brokers and platforms**: client assets, trading activity, and net interest income on client cash, which falls as rates drop and as clients shift into higher-yield products.
- **Investment banks and capital markets**: M&A and issuance cycles, trading revenue volatility, and compensation ratio.
- **Exchanges**: trading and clearing volumes, volatility, and the share of recurring revenue from data, indexes and listings; high margins and network moats, but regulatory fee scrutiny.
- **Data providers**: subscription retention, pricing power and AI-driven disruption of workflows.
- **Valuation**: P/E and FCF yield; premium for recurring fees over cyclical activity; check the capital returned and any seed-capital investment that adds market risk.

Record flows and revenue at market highs often reverse together; judge fee quality on a down-market year, not an up-market one.
