When analysing an industrial or capital goods company, first place it on the map (short-cycle vs long-cycle machinery, electrical and automation, engineering and construction, building products, distribution, conglomerate) — cyclicality and visibility differ:

- **Orders and backlog**: orders lead revenue; book-to-bill above one means growth ahead, below one means a slowdown. Check backlog conversion time and margin of the backlog.
- **Pricing vs cost**: price realisation against material, labour and freight inflation; pricing that stays ahead of cost supports margin.
- **Incremental margins**: change in profit divided by change in sales; high incrementals on recovery, and equally high decrementals in a downturn.
- **Aftermarket and service**: recurring service, parts and software share dampens cycles and carries higher margins; a rising service mix warrants a higher multiple.
- **Cycle exposure**: end-market mix (construction, agriculture, mining, oil and gas, manufacturing) and the point in each cycle.
- **Structural demand**: electrification, grid, automation and data-center buildout lift electrical names; check capacity additions, so demand is not met with oversupply.
- **Engineering and construction**: fixed-price contract risk, margin fade, and working capital swings.
- **Working capital and balance sheet**: inventory, receivables, and customer advances; free cash flow conversion near net income is a quality sign.
- **Valuation**: EV/EBITDA and P/E on mid-cycle earnings; for conglomerates, a sum of the parts and holding discount.

Peak margins look like structural gains until orders roll over; test whether margin is price-driven or only volume-driven.
