When analysing a medtech or diagnostics company, first place it on the map (implants and surgical devices, consumables and supplies, diagnostics and testing, life-science tools, health IT/services) — growth drivers and cyclicality differ:

- **Procedure volumes**: growth tied to hospital procedure counts, ageing demographics and elective-surgery backlogs; new indications widen the market.
- **Recurring revenue**: razor/razorblade model, installed base of systems with consumables and service. A high recurring share gives steadier earnings and a higher multiple.
- **Innovation and share**: R&D intensity, pipeline of next-generation products, and competitive position in categories with few players; surgical robots and diabetes tech reward early leaders.
- **Regulation and reimbursement**: regulatory pathway (510(k), PMA in the US, CE/MDR in the EU), approval delays and recalls; reimbursement or coding changes can swing volume.
- **Diagnostics and tools**: testing volume normalisation after surges, pricing for tests, pharma and biotech customer funding as a demand driver for tools.
- **Margins and cost**: gross margin by product mix, hospital pricing power, and supply chain inflation.
- **Health IT**: contract renewals, annual recurring revenue growth and net retention.
- **Valuation**: EV/EBITDA and P/E against growth; premium multiples hold only when organic growth remains steady, so look at organic, ex-acquisition growth.

Acquisitions and adjusted-earnings add-backs are common in this group; compare adjusted numbers with free cash flow to see how much of the earnings is real.
