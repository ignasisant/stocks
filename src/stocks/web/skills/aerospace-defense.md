When analysing an aerospace or defense company, first place it on the map (airframer, engine maker, tier-1/2 supplier, defense prime, space, services) — the mix of commercial and defense changes growth, margins and risk:

- **Backlog**: size, years of revenue covered and book-to-bill above roughly one for growth; check how much is firm vs options and cancellable.
- **Commercial vs defense mix**: commercial depends on airline traffic and aircraft deliveries; defense on government budgets and long programs.
- **Aftermarket**: spares and services are far more profitable than original equipment and tied to fleet hours flown; engine makers earn largely there.
- **Production ramps**: delivery rate targets, supply-chain bottlenecks (castings, engines, labour), and quality issues that halt deliveries or inspections.
- **Program risk**: fixed-price development contracts can cause large charges; check recent write-downs and the share of such contracts.
- **Budget cycles**: US defense appropriations, NATO and European rearmament spending, and export approvals; multi-year contracts give visibility but budget fights cause delays.
- **Cash conversion**: free cash flow as a percentage of net income, customer advances and pension liabilities.
- **Valuation**: EV/EBIT and FCF yield; aftermarket-heavy franchises justify premiums, while prime contractors trade on stable FCF and capital returns.

A record backlog is not a record profit: check the margin embedded in the backlog and whether the supply chain can deliver it.
