When analysing a consumer staples company, first place it on the map (beverages, packaged food, household and personal care, tobacco, agricultural commodities) — growth quality and risks differ:

- **Volume vs price/mix**: organic growth split into volume and price/mix. Price-led growth fades once consumers trade down; volume declines are the sign of lost demand.
- **Pricing power**: brand strength against private label share and retailer bargaining. Gains in private label after inflation spells show where brands are losing.
- **Input costs and margin**: commodity exposure (cocoa, sugar, grains, packaging, energy), hedging and lag in passing costs on; gross margin recovery after inflation is a point to test.
- **Emerging markets**: growth and mix benefit, offset by currency devaluation; check FX effects on reported vs organic growth.
- **Category risks**: GLP-1 obesity drugs may reduce snack and sugary-food volumes; health trends hit tobacco volumes (offset by pricing and next-generation products) and sugar-heavy beverages.
- **Payout and balance sheet**: dividend cover by free cash flow, net debt / EBITDA, and acquisition track record.
- **Valuation**: P/E and FCF yield, with the premium set against the bond yield; defensive multiples compress when yields rise.
- **Commodity producers**: farm products follow crop prices and weather, not brands.

A staple with a high yield and shrinking volumes can be cheap for a reason; check whether dividend growth rests on price rises alone.
