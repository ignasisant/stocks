When analysing a retailer or restaurant company, first place it on the map (discount and grocery, specialty and apparel, department stores, e-commerce, dealerships, franchised vs company-operated restaurants) — margin and risk differ:

- **Same-store sales**: comparable sales growth split into traffic and ticket. Growth from traffic is healthier than from price increases alone.
- **Gross margin and inventory**: inventory turns, markdown rates and shrink. Inventory growing faster than sales precedes margin-damaging discounting, especially in apparel and department stores.
- **Unit economics**: new-store sales and payback, cash-on-cash returns, maturity curves; unit growth in saturated markets cannibalises existing stores.
- **E-commerce**: online share of sales, fulfilment costs, returns, and profitability of the online channel; marketplaces judged on take rate and gross merchandise value.
- **Restaurants**: franchised models are asset-light and earn royalties; company-owned models carry restaurant-level margin and labour and commodity cost exposure.
- **Leases**: leases are debt-like, so use lease-adjusted leverage (net debt plus lease liabilities against EBITDAR) and watch store closure costs.
- **Consumer exposure**: income tier, discretionary vs staples, credit availability for dealers and big-ticket items.
- **Valuation**: P/E and EV/EBITDAR against growth and returns on capital; compare against peers of the same format.

Strong comparable sales on promotions can hide weak margins; check gross margin and inventory quality alongside the headline.
