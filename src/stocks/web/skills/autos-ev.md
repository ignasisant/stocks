When analysing an auto company, first place it on the map (legacy OEM, EV pure-play, luxury, supplier tier 1/2, recreational vehicles) — economics and risks differ:

- **Volumes**: deliveries vs production, inventory at dealers and days supply; production ahead of sales ends in incentives and write-downs.
- **Price and mix**: average selling price, incentives and the share of trucks and SUVs; discounting to protect volume signals weak demand.
- **EV transition**: EV share of mix, EV gross margin per vehicle (several legacy OEMs lose money on EVs), battery cost and supply, software and autonomy claims treated as unproven until revenue appears.
- **Regulatory credits**: emissions credits can be a large share of an EV maker's profit; their decline would expose underlying margin.
- **Capex and R&D**: heavy and recurring; compare against FCF through the cycle. Suppliers face customer-driven volume risk and fixed-price contracts.
- **Captive finance**: the finance arm inflates debt; separate industrial net debt from financial services debt when judging leverage.
- **Trade and competition**: tariffs, local-content rules, and low-cost Chinese brands gaining share in Europe and emerging markets.
- **Cyclicality**: demand follows rates, credit and consumer confidence; recreational vehicles are more discretionary still.
- **Valuation**: mid-cycle EV/EBIT and FCF yield, P/B for OEMs; EV pure-plays are valued on growth and need cash runway checks.

Low P/E at peak margin is typical before a downturn; judge OEMs on through-cycle earnings and recall or warranty history.
