When analysing a hardware or devices company, first place it on the map (branded devices, PCs and servers, networking and telecom equipment, components and contract manufacturing, test and scientific instruments) — margin structure and cyclicality differ:

- **Units and ASP**: shipments and average selling price separately. Growth from price/mix on premium products is sturdier than growth from volume bought with discounts.
- **Product cycle**: refresh timing, installed-base upgrade rates, and dependence on one flagship. Commodity hardware has thin margins; ecosystems and services raise them.
- **Gross margin**: watch component costs (memory, chips), tariffs and freight. Margin expansion from services or software attach rate is worth more than from a temporary cost dip.
- **Inventory and channel**: inventory days, backlog and book-to-bill; telecom and enterprise networking see order surges followed by digestion once customers have stock.
- **Customers**: carrier, enterprise or hyperscaler concentration; component makers often depend on one or two OEMs.
- **Supply chain**: single-source parts, manufacturing in a few countries, and exposure to export rules.
- **Valuation**: P/E and EV/EBIT on mid-cycle margins; for instruments, recurring consumables and service revenue justify a premium. Cheap multiples on peak-cycle margins are a warning, not a bargain.
- **Balance sheet**: net cash is common; check buybacks against free cash flow and working-capital swings.

Pandemic-era or AI-driven demand surges can pull forward purchases; treat the first slowdown as a possible digestion phase rather than a trend break, but verify with inventory data.
