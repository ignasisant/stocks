When analysing a pharma company, first place it on the map (global large-cap, specialty, generics and biosimilars, animal health) — the main question is how far revenue growth is protected through the next patent cliff:

- **Patent cliff**: list top products as a share of sales with loss-of-exclusivity (LOE) year and the revenue exposed. Sales usually fall steeply after generics or biosimilars arrive; check the combined hit through the next five years.
- **Pipeline refill**: late-stage assets and their peak-sales potential against revenue lost; discount early-stage claims and rely on phase 3 and filed assets.
- **Franchise concentration**: a few blockbusters (obesity/GLP-1-like, oncology, immunology) can dominate growth. Supply capacity, competitive entrants and payer coverage drive these franchises' trajectory.
- **Pricing pressure**: US Medicare price negotiation under the IRA, rebates and gross-to-net erosion, reference pricing and cost control in Europe.
- **R&D productivity**: R&D spend against approvals and returns, and reliance on acquisitions; large deals can destroy value if overpaid.
- **Generics and biosimilars**: volume-driven, thin margins, price erosion in mature markets; check manufacturing quality and pipeline of complex products.
- **Balance sheet and returns**: net debt after deals, dividend cover by free cash flow, buybacks.
- **Valuation**: P/E and FCF yield on post-cliff earnings, plus a sum-of-the-parts of products; a low P/E before a cliff is typically fair.

Check litigation reserves and pricing legislation; a single policy ruling can reset the earnings base of a whole franchise.
