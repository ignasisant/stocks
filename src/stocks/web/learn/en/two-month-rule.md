You sell at a loss to offset gains and, the next day, buy the same stock back. Can you claim the loss? In Spain, usually not.

## The idea
The anti-avoidance rule in **article 33.5.f of the LIRPF** stops you deducting a loss if you buy homogeneous securities (of the same company) in the **two months before or after** the sale. It applies to listed securities. For unlisted securities the period is one year.

The loss is **not lost: it is deferred**. It stays "frozen" and is added to the loss, or subtracted from the gain, when you sell the shares you bought back, with no repurchase nearby.

It is the Spanish equivalent of the US *wash sale rule*, which uses a 30-day window.

The rule affects only the repurchased part: if you sell 100 shares at a loss and buy back 10, only a tenth of the loss is deferred.

## An example
- 1 March: you buy 100 shares at €50 = €5,000.
- 15 September: you sell them at €40 = €4,000. Loss: €1,000.
- 20 October: you buy back 100 shares at €41 = €4,100. That is within two months, so the €1,000 loss is **not computable** this year.
- 15 January the following year: you sell those 100 shares at €45 = €4,500.

That sale gives a gain of 4,500 − 4,100 = €400. But now you can apply the deferred loss: 400 − 1,000 = **−€600** computable result, in the year of that sale.

The total amount is the same. Only **when** you can use it changes.

## The common mistake
Thinking that waiting a few days is enough. The window is two full months, before and after. Buying **before** the sale counts too: if you add more the month before and then sell at a loss, part of that loss is deferred.

Another trap: the rule looks at the same security. Buying a different one, even a similar one, does not trigger it; but decide based on your strategy, not just the tax. In doubtful cases, consult a tax adviser.
