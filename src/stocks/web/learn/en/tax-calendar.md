Tax is not paid the day you sell. It is calculated on the whole year and filed months later. Knowing the calendar lets you decide in time.

## The idea
The **tax year** is the calendar year: 1 January to 31 December. Everything you sell or receive in those twelve months goes into the same return.

The **tax return** (IRPF) is filed in the following year's campaign, which usually runs from **April to the end of June** (it closes on 30 June). Exact dates shift a little each year, so confirm them on the tax agency's website. If you split the payment, the second instalment is usually 5 November.

**Forms 720 and 721** have their own deadline: up to 31 March.

## The draft may be incomplete
The tax office gives you a draft with the data sent by **Spanish** institutions. A foreign broker does not send it. If you sold shares or received dividends there, the draft does not include them and you must add them yourself.

Keep throughout the year:

- annual statements and trade confirmations;
- the commissions paid;
- gross dividends and the withholding at source;
- the exchange rate for each date, if you trade in other currencies.

## An example
In November you have €12,000 of realised gains and a position with −€3,000 unrealised.

- Without selling: you are taxed on €12,000 = 6,000 × 19 % + 6,000 × 21 % = 1,140 + 1,260 = **€2,400**.
- Selling before 31 December: 12,000 − 3,000 = €9,000 = 1,140 + 3,000 × 21 % = 1,140 + 630 = **€1,770**.

You save €630. If you sell on 2 January, the loss falls into the next year and does not offset these gains.

## The common mistake
Deciding at the last minute. Remember also that the two-month rule crosses the year-end: selling at a loss on 20 December and buying back in January defers that loss.

And do not wait until June to sort your documents. If your case is complex, consult an adviser.
