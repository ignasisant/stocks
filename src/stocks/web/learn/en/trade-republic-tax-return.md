Trade Republic is a German broker: it withholds no Spanish tax and sends nothing to the Spanish tax agency. The return is yours to prepare. These are the steps.

## What to download
In the app, open your profile documents and download the **account statement** for the whole period, from your first purchase. To know which shares you sold, **FIFO** needs the price of every purchase, even years old ones.

Trade Republic also offers an annual tax report. It is built for Germany: do not copy it into your return as it is, because its cost method and the taxes it nets out may not be the Spanish ones.

## What to check
- **Sales.** Each sale is taxed in the savings base. The gain is what you receive minus what you paid for **the oldest shares**, using the price and fees of each trade.
- **Dividends.** The return takes the **gross** amount. If you collect US dividends with a W-8BEN, the statement is already net of the 15 %; you recover that withholding through the **double-taxation deduction**.
- **Interest on cash.** Interest Trade Republic pays on your cash is capital income, also in the savings base.
- **Losses.** If you sold at a loss and rebought the same security inside the **two-month rule** window, that loss cannot be used yet.

## An example
You buy 10 shares at €100 in January and 10 more at €120 in March. In December you sell 10 at €130. FIFO sells the January ones: gain of 10 × (130 − 100) = **€300**, before fees. The March shares stay in your portfolio at a cost of €120.

## Form 720
Your securities are held outside Spain. If their value, added to your other foreign accounts and assets, is over **€50,000** on 31 December, file **form 720** by 31 March. Cash in the account counts too.

## The typical mistake
Trusting the broker's report, or declaring only the sales. The gross dividends, the interest and the losses the two-month rule blocks get forgotten.
