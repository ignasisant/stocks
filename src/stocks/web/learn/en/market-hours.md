Markets are not open all the time. When they close, the price stands still, but the news keeps coming.

## The idea
The **session** is the period when an exchange matches orders. Spanish time:

- **BME (Madrid) and Xetra (Frankfurt)**: 9:00 to 17:30.
- **NYSE and Nasdaq**: normally 15:30 to 22:00. For two weeks a year, when the US and Europe change their clocks on different dates, the schedule shifts by one hour.

Before and after the regular session, some brokers let you trade in **pre-market** and **after-hours**. These are periods with low volume, wide spreads and more volatile prices. That is why limit orders make sense.

Exchanges also close on **market holidays**, which do not match your country's holidays. One day can have Frankfurt open and New York closed.

## An example
A company closes at €50.00 and publishes bad results while the market is closed. The next day it opens at €46.00.

- Jump: €46.00 − €50.00 = −€4.00.
- As a percentage: −4 / 50 = **−8 %**.

That jump with no prices in between is an **opening gap**. Nobody could sell at €48: no trades happened in between. This is why the earnings calendar matters: gaps often come after it.

Another case: on Good Friday the Spanish exchange is closed. If you look at your portfolio, the "yesterday" price does not move. It is not a bug: there was no session.

## The common mistake
Believing the price you see at 8:00 is the opening price. Before the session there are only pre-market quotes, with little liquidity, which can change a lot at the open.

People also assume a stop protects them overnight. It does not trigger while the market is closed: if there is a gap, it executes at the open, at a worse price than the one you set.
