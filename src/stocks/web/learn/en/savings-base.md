You sell shares at a profit and the tax office wants its share. But someone who makes €3,000 does not pay the same rate as someone who makes €70,000: the tax works in brackets.

## The idea
In Spain, **IRPF** (personal income tax) splits your income into two baskets. Salary goes into the general base. Investment gains (plus dividends and interest) go into the **savings base** (base del ahorro), which has its own rates.

A **capital gain** (ganancia patrimonial) is the difference between what you get when you sell and what it cost you to buy. It only exists when you sell: a rise you have not sold is not taxed.

From 2025, the savings-base brackets are:

- up to €6,000: 19 %
- €6,000 to €50,000: 21 %
- €50,000 to €200,000: 23 %
- €200,000 to €300,000: 27 %
- above €300,000: 30 %

These are **marginal** rates: each rate applies only to the euros that fall inside its bracket, not to the whole total.

## An example
Lucía ends the year with €70,000 of net gain. It is calculated bracket by bracket:

- €6,000 × 19 % = €1,140
- €44,000 × 21 % = €9,240
- €20,000 × 23 % = €4,600

Total: **€14,980**, an average of 21.4 %. She does not pay 23 % on the whole €70,000, because that rate only touches the last €20,000.

## Funds: a transfer is not taxed
There is a well-known exception. If you sell an **investment fund** and use the money to buy another fund through a **transfer** (traspaso, where the fund manager moves the money directly), you pay no tax on the gain. The tax is deferred until you actually redeem.

This does **not** apply to shares or ETFs. Even though an ETF is a fund, it trades on an exchange: selling one and buying another is a sale, and the gain is taxed that year.

## The common mistake
Believing that "jumping a bracket" makes you pay more on everything you earned. It does not: only the last euro pays the highest rate.

Another frequent error: forgetting that selling one ETF to buy a similar one triggers tax, while doing the same with transferred funds does not. Before moving money between products, check which kind they are. For doubtful cases, consult an adviser.
