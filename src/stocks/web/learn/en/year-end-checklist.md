The tax year ends on 31 December. Whatever you sell up to that day counts for your 2026 return, filed in the April to June 2027 campaign. If you want to use a loss this year, decide now, not in the last week.

## The dates
- **Mid-December:** the comfortable margin. Markets are thinner, but there is still time if something goes wrong.
- **31 December 2026 (Thursday):** the last day. The trade date counts, not the settlement date. Some exchanges close early or that very day: check their calendar and do not leave it to the end.
- **The two-month window:** if you sell at a loss on 10 December, you should not have bought the same security since 10 October and cannot do so until 10 February 2027. Each sale date has its own window.
- **April to June 2027:** the tax return campaign. **31 March 2027:** deadline for forms 720 and 721.

## The steps
- Add up the gains you have already realised this year, across all your brokers.
- List the positions at a loss and, for each, the unrealised loss in euros.
- Check the window: did you buy that security in the previous two months? Is there a scheduled purchase or a recurring plan that would buy it again?
- Sell with margin and keep each trade confirmation.
- Do not buy the same security again until the window has passed. If you want to stay invested, look at something similar but not identical.

## An example
You have realised gains of €8,000 in 2026 and a position with an unrealised loss of €2,500 that you have not touched for months.

- Without selling: €8,000 = 6,000 × 19 % + 2,000 × 21 % = 1,140 + 420 = **€1,560**.
- Selling before 31 December: €5,500 × 19 % = **€1,045**.

You save €515, but it is a deferral: your cost basis is lower if you buy again later.

## The typical mistake
Selling at a loss and letting a monthly investment plan buy the same security again in January. The window also looks forward, and that loss is deferred. Pause automatic purchases of that security before you sell.

If your case mixes years or products, ask an adviser.
