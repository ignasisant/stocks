You receive a dividend from a US company and it arrives already trimmed. Do you pay twice? No: there is a deduction to avoid that.

## The idea
In Spain, dividends are taxed in the **savings base**, on their **gross** amount, at the same brackets as gains (19 % up to €6,000).

Before reaching you, the company's country usually keeps a **withholding at source**. In the US it is 30 % by default, but the treaty with Spain lowers it to **15 %** if you have signed the **W-8BEN** form with your broker. Other countries withhold more or less depending on their treaty (*tax treaty*).

On top of that, if you are paid through a Spanish entity, it applies a **19 %** withholding on the gross amount. With a foreign broker there is no Spanish withholding: you declare it yourself in your return.

To avoid paying twice on the same income, the **international double-taxation deduction** subtracts what you paid abroad from your Spanish tax. It has a cap: the **lower** of the tax paid abroad (within the treaty limit) and the Spanish tax that corresponds to that dividend.

## An example
You receive €1,000 gross from a US company, with a W-8BEN and a foreign broker.

- US withholding (15 %): **€150**. You receive €850.
- Spanish tax at 19 %: 1,000 × 19 % = **€190**.
- Deduction: the lower of €150 paid and €190 of Spanish tax = **€150**.
- You pay the tax office: 190 − 150 = **€40**.

Total tax: 150 + 40 = €190. That is the usual 19 %, split between two countries.

## The common mistake
Not signing the W-8BEN. Without it, 30 % is withheld: €300. In Spain you can only deduct up to the treaty limit (€150); the other €150 must be reclaimed from the US tax authority, a slow process.

Other mistakes: declaring the net instead of the gross, or believing the withholding already settles your obligation. With a foreign broker, nobody files for you. For cases involving several countries, consult an adviser.
