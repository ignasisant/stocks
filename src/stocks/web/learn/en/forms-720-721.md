If you keep your shares with a foreign broker, the tax office does not receive that information on its own. Forms 720 and 721 exist so that you provide it.

## The idea
**Modelo 720** is an informative declaration of assets and rights abroad. It has three separate blocks: bank accounts, securities (shares, funds, ETFs held with a foreign institution) and real estate. You must file it if the value of **one block** exceeds **€50,000** on 31 December.

**Modelo 721** is the same, but for **crypto assets held in custody outside Spain** (for example, on a foreign exchange): you file it if they exceed €50,000 on 31 December.

The deadline for both is **January to March** of the following year (up to 31 March). They are **informative: you pay nothing** for filing them.

After the first year, the 720 only has to be filed again if a block grows by more than €20,000 compared with the last one declared.

## An example
Marta holds, on 31 December:

- €62,000 in shares with a foreign broker.
- €20,000 in a foreign bank account.

Each block is looked at separately. The securities exceed €50,000, so she files the 720 for the securities block. The account (€20,000) does not reach the limit and is left out. Different blocks are not added together to reach €50,000.

## The penalties
The 720 had a very harsh penalty regime. The EU Court of Justice ruled it contrary to European law on **27 January 2022**, and **Law 5/2022** corrected the disproportionate fines. That does not remove the obligation: filing late or not filing still carries ordinary penalties.

## The common mistake
Thinking that, because you pay nothing, it can be ignored. The obligation is still in force even though the penalty is smaller.

Another slip: believing that a Spanish broker holding foreign shares obliges you to file the 720. What counts is where the security is **held in custody**, not where it is listed. Consult an adviser if you are unsure about your case.
